# Little Witches in the Wood - Exploration Storyboard

Alternate working title: Little Witches in Da Hood.

Revised direction: a first-person cooperative exploration and crafting adventure for 1-4 players. The following is a proposed design, not a description of implemented game systems.

## Core Idea

Apprentice witches inherit Grandpa's guidebook and an abandoned cottage on a floating woodland island. They venture into forests, caves, and marshes to gather materials, discover recipes, and prepare for the creatures guarding the island's ancient magic. The Frog King is the final boss guarding the Heartseed and the path to ascension.

Minecraft is the reference for player freedom, gathering, preparation, and progression. Players choose where to explore and what to make. A voxel world, procedural terrain, and unrestricted building are separate scope decisions, not requirements for this concept.

The core loop is:

Explore -> gather materials -> craft equipment and brew potions -> reach new places and fight enemies -> defeat bosses -> gain new crafting possibilities -> explore further.

The story supports these activities through landmarks, Grandpa's notes, and discoveries. It does not rely on the King repeatedly assigning collection requests.

## Story Foundation

Grandpa was the greatest alchemist and an explorer of the floating islands. His guidebook records ingredient habitats, strange creatures, and unfinished experiments. Several pages are missing, but their locations are hinted at by sketches of landmarks.

The island's Heartseed once powered a passage to the sky above. The Frog King seized it and now draws strength from its magic. Grandpa left clues about the guardians and ways to challenge them. The apprentices must learn the island, build their own equipment, and reclaim the Heartseed.

The guidebook is a field journal and recipe reference. Inspecting resources, discovering pages, and testing recipes expand the team's knowledge. It offers leads without prescribing the next activity.

## Storyboard Beats

These describe a possible player journey. Exploration, gathering, and preparation can happen in different orders; they are not a compulsory sequence of missions.

### 1. A Cottage Above the Clouds

Visual: The witches step out of Grandpa's cottage. A forest spreads below a rocky ridge, mist rises from a distant marsh, and the Frog King's crown glows above a ruined tower.

Story: Grandpa has disappeared, leaving the cottage, basic supplies, and his guidebook.

Book line: "The world will not hand you its secrets. Pack a bag and go find them."

Player action: Inspect the brewing station and crafting bench, gather nearby wood and stone, and choose a destination. The home base is a place to return and prepare.

### 2. The First Expedition

Visual: One witch gathers mushrooms beneath a log while another discovers a cave. A third spots a ruined watchtower through the trees.

Story: The book's sketches match landmarks in the world. Each discovery suggests possibilities: better tools, new recipes, or a route to rare materials.

Player action: Gather wood, stone, herbs, and common mushrooms. Find enough materials to craft a basic gathering tool, a wand, and potion containers. Establish a useful first healing recipe.

Design purpose: Give early materials immediate uses. Players can follow a curiosity, gather toward a recipe, or scout somewhere dangerous without waiting for a quest.

### 3. Something Worth Coming Back For

Visual: A sparkling ore vein sits behind tough cave rock. A rare flower grows above a broken bridge. Giant footprints lead into a root-covered clearing.

Story: The island contains resources the apprentices cannot yet collect safely. Grandpa's nearby notes mention stronger tools, jump brews, and a creature called the Root Guardian.

Player action: Mark discoveries and bring materials home. Craft a stronger gathering tool or brew a traversal potion, then return to reach the resource.

Design purpose: Let players see an opportunity before acquiring the means to use it. New equipment changes what they can do in places they already know.

### 4. Make Your Own Answer

Visual: The cottage bench is covered in tools and bottles. Someone tests a jump potion outside while another bottles healing brews.

Story: Grandpa's recipes are starting points. The witches decide how to prepare for their discoveries.

Player action: Craft persistent tools, wands, and equipment at the bench. Brew consumable healing, traversal, defense, and attack effects at the cauldron. Successful recipes enter the shared book.

Examples: Wood and stone make a gathering tool; Moon Ore improves a wand; herbs and dew make healing; Bouncecaps and wind flowers make a jump brew. Exact ingredients and balance remain provisional.

Design purpose: Separate crafting equipment from brewing effects. Several recipes should share materials so players make useful choices about how to spend supplies.

### 5. The First Guardian

Visual: A huge creature of bark and roots rises in a clearing. Its glowing core is briefly exposed after a heavy slam.

Story: Grandpa's field note gives a clue: "Watch what it protects when it moves."

Player action: Discover the boss while exploring. Observe or attempt the fight, retreat to prepare if necessary, and return with crafted weapons and potions. Dodge roots, attack the exposed core, and help fallen teammates.

Preparation options: Fire brews weaken bark, jump brews help evade ground attacks, and healing supports a longer fight. A basic wand provides a renewable attack so empty potion stocks do not make victory impossible.

Reward: A Guardian Ember unlocks stronger crafting recipes and lets players clear specific enchanted barriers to further resources. It is a permanent progression material, not consumable boss ammunition.

### 6. Beyond the Familiar Wood

Visual: The witches venture into glowing caves and a misty marsh. The familiar forest remains visible from higher ledges.

Story: Recovered pages explain that the Frog King uses the stolen Heartseed to feed his growing power. The ruined tower is both his lair and the entrance to the sky gate.

Player action: Discover rarer materials, upgrade equipment, and experiment with counters to marsh creatures. Optional ruins contain recipe clues and Grandpa's history. Ordinary routes should permit exploration across regions; selected resources and shortcuts require better tools or boss rewards.

Design purpose: Expand the player's choices. A biome changes resources, hazards, and possible recipes rather than just increasing enemy health.

### 7. Preparing for His Royal Croakness

Visual: From a cliff, players see the Frog King leap across his arena and lash out with his tongue. Back home, they lay out bottles and compare equipment.

Story: The guidebook warns that his crown draws power from the Heartseed. An observation page suggests ways to interrupt it.

Player action: Choose equipment and consumables, scout the arena, and decide when to challenge him. Friends can take temporary roles such as attacker, healer, or supplier without fixed character classes.

Possible preparations: Sticky brews slow leaps; defensive brews soften tongue hits; shock bottles briefly interrupt the crown; healing helps recover from mistakes. These provide advantages, with several viable ways to win.

Design purpose: Make earlier exploration matter in the final fight. Discovering a clue or gathering an unusual ingredient should have a visible combat payoff.

### 8. The Frog King's Fall

Visual: The King slams into the marsh, sending rings across the water. Potion bottles burst against his crown as witches dodge, attack, and revive each other.

Dialogue: "You arrive in MY kingdom carrying homemade soup?!"

Player action: Fight a cooperative boss with readable tongue strikes, leaps, and crown attacks. Exploit the openings created by dodging and well-timed brews. The crown breaks when the King is defeated, releasing the Heartseed.

Story outcome: Reclaiming the Heartseed is the essential victory. A later story expansion can let the group kill the weakened King or use a discovered cure to spare him after the fight. Both preserve boss combat as the climax.

### 9. Ascension

Visual: The witches restore the Heartseed to the ruined sky gate. Leaves and cloud spiral upward, revealing another floating island.

Story: Grandpa's book reveals a final message recognizing their growth from apprentices into witches.

Book line: "Now you know what I meant by learning from the world."

Player action: Activate the gate and complete this island. The next island is a sequel hook; it does not need to exist in the initial prototype.

## Exploration and Multiplayer Rules

- Use recognizable landmarks and distinct ingredient habitats. Shuffle resource positions for replayability only after the basic exploration works.
- Make discoveries useful through new recipes, gathering opportunities, traversal options, equipment, or boss preparation.
- Start with fixed cottage crafting and brewing stations plus shared storage. Freeform base building is an optional later feature.
- Share discovered recipes and journal entries while letting players carry their own supplies. Provide map markers or pings alongside proximity voice.
- Scale boss encounters for 1-4 players. Flexible roles should never require a specific class or multiple simultaneous operators.
- Make essential consumable materials renewable. Preserve equipment and recipe knowledge on defeat; use recoverable dropped supplies and a return to the cottage so failure leads to another expedition.
- Distinguish tools and gear from expendable potions. Keep a basic attack available without rare consumables.

## Scope for This Repository

Existing foundations: Unity 6 first-person movement, lobby and Relay flow, proximity voice, and networked object pickup, rotation, and tossing. These can support gathering and physical potion interactions.

Required additions: harvestable resources, inventory and storage, recipes, a crafting bench, shared brewing, potion effects, enemies and health, boss behavior, equipment progression, respawn, and a shared world progression state. Persistent saved worlds can follow after the first complete playable loop.

First playable milestone: a cottage, a forest with six resource types, one gathering tool, one wand, healing and offensive brews, and one boss. Players explore, bring materials home, prepare, and win a boss fight together. Its reward activates a gate to demonstrate progression.

Expanded storyboard scope: add a cave or marsh, traversal brews, equipment upgrades, the Root Guardian, and the Frog King finale. Keep the initial world authored; large procedural islands and freeform building are separate projects within the larger game vision.
